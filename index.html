About

Marketing Scholar & Educator, Rooted in Louisiana

Dr. Britton R. Leggett is a Louisianian and Assistant Professor of Marketing at McNeese State University in Lake Charles, where he holds the JPMorgan Chase Professorship in Business and received the College of Business Outstanding Teacher Award in 2026. He spent twenty years teaching in northeast Louisiana schools before moving into higher education full time, and he holds an MBA and an M.Ed. from Louisiana Tech.

His research follows the evidence, not the narrative. He studies the forces reshaping Louisiana, from data centers and carbon capture to tariffs and grocery prices, and tests what companies, officials, and the press claim about them. His work appears in the Journal of Marketing Theory and Practice, the Journal of Marketing Education, and the Journal of Marketing Analytics, and he holds a Ph.D. in Marketing from the University of South Alabama.

In the classroom, he teaches selling, research, and analytics, and he is fully invested in teaching students the right way to use AI: ethically, transparently, and as a tool that sharpens their research and job skills instead of replacing their thinking. He has leveraged AI to build simulators and case studies for his students, and likewise leveraged AI’s power to connect students with potential employers.

Away from campus, you’ll find him fishing the salmon run in Alaska or in a Louisiana deer stand during the rut.

The Louisiana Professor · McNeese · Lake Charles

Common sense. Balanced. Questioning everyone.

Louisiana is changing fast. Billions in new industry, from carbon capture to data centers to space, are arriving in our parishes, and families are feeling tariffs and grocery prices at the checkout. Every one of those stories comes with an official explanation, from companies, from government, and from the press. My work doesn’t take any of them at face value. I check everyone’s claims against the data, with the rigor of peer-reviewed research, follow the evidence rather than the narrative, and put what I find to work for Louisiana businesses and McNeese students.

Current Projects

The Louisiana Research Agenda

The questions I’m working on right now, most of them about Louisiana. Findings will be posted here once the work completes peer review.

Data Centers

When a data center comes to a Louisiana parish, what earns, or loses, community acceptance?

Large projects like the one in Richland Parish promise jobs and investment while drawing on water, power, and tax incentives. This work examines how companies make their case and how residents judge whether the deal is fair.

In progress
Carbon Capture & Energy

How do Louisianans weigh the promises and risks of carbon capture and storage?

Louisiana is central to the country’s carbon capture build-out. This project studies how the technology is explained to the public and what drives support or opposition.

In progress
Economic Development

What should Louisiana expect when a megaproject announces itself?

The proposed spaceport in Vermilion Parish arrived with big numbers on jobs and coastal investment. This work compares how benefits are described by different voices and how the public reads those claims.

In progress
Tariffs & Pricing

When prices rise, how should a business explain why?

Tariffs pushed many companies to raise prices. This research looks at how firms communicate those increases and how customers react to different kinds of explanations.

In progress
Food Prices

Why do beef and grocery prices feel unfair, and does the common story hold up?

Imports, meatpacker concentration, and country-of-origin labeling all shape what families pay. This project separates what the data show from the narratives people hear.

In progress
Teachers & Public Pay

Where did Louisiana teachers’ raises actually go?

A plain-language look at how a decade of state pay raises compares with rising insurance and living costs, written for educators and policymakers.

In progress
AI & Students

Are paid AI tools creating a new divide among college students?

Free and premium AI tools are not the same. This work asks who pays for the better versions, who can’t, and what that means for learning and opportunity at regional universities.

In progress
Community Technology

How are license-plate camera networks sold to the communities that adopt them?

Automated license-plate readers are spreading through cities and neighborhoods. This project studies how the technology is pitched, the public conversation around it, and what it means for trust and privacy.

In progress

Scholarship

Research Interests, Built to Benefit Louisiana

Every area of my research aims at something Louisiana needs: stronger businesses, better-prepared graduates, and honest, evidence-based conversations about the industries and prices changing our state.

Energy, Industry & Public Trust

How carbon capture, petrochemical, and data center developers earn, or lose, the trust of Louisiana communities, and what fair, transparent communication with residents looks like.

Economic Development & Community Acceptance

Testing the promises that come with big projects, including jobs, investment, and incentives, against the evidence, so parishes and taxpayers can judge the deals on the merits.

Prices, Tariffs & Louisiana Consumers

Why prices rise, how businesses explain it, and how customers respond. Practical guidance for Louisiana retailers and small businesses, and clearer facts for families at the checkout.

AI for Louisiana Business

How AI tools reshape selling, marketing, and the customer journey, and how small and mid-sized Louisiana firms can use them without the hype or the big-company budget.

Preparing Louisiana’s Workforce

Marketing and sales education that produces job-ready graduates, from AI-assisted experiential learning to the paid-versus-free AI divide among students at regional universities like McNeese.

Sales, Ethics & Customer Relationships

What makes salespeople effective and honest, and how unethical sales cultures damage customer relationships. Lessons that matter in a relationship-driven state where business still runs on a handshake.

Sports, Brands & Local Loyalty

Brand equity and fan loyalty in sports, with lessons for Louisiana’s college athletics, minor-league, and community sports organizations competing for attendance and support.

Consumer Trust, Privacy & Safety

How surveillance technology, online platforms, and new products are sold to communities, and how Louisianans can weigh safety, privacy, and trust for themselves.
